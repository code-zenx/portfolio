The dashboard said p95 was 180ms and flat for a month. A customer said the
product was unusable. Both were true.

## Aggregates erase tenants

A percentile over all requests answers "how slow is a typical request." It
does not answer "is anyone having a bad time," and those are different
questions the moment your traffic is not uniform.

Our largest tenant was 2% of request volume. They could be at four seconds
across the board and never move the global p95 by a millisecond.

## Slice before you summarize

The change was one label:

```go
latency.With(prometheus.Labels{
    "tenant": req.TenantID,
    "route":  route,
}).Observe(elapsed.Seconds())
```

Cardinality is the usual objection, and it is a real one — this is fine at
hundreds of tenants and a disaster at millions. For the millions case,
aggregate to a per-tenant *max* on a rolling window and alert on that instead.

## The number worth watching

We now alert on the worst tenant's p95, not the global one. It is a noisier
signal and it has caught three incidents that the global view slept through.

The general form: when you summarize across a dimension your users can feel,
you have to keep at least one statistic that survives the summary.
